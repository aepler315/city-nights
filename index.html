ABOUT THIS VIEW

A little perspective.

City Nights is an ideal-clear, first-person observatory. The Earth is curved. Terrain and building geometry obstruct the view, and distant objects retain their actual angular scale.

Real geography
Cesium World Terrain and OSM Buildings. Building coverage, heights, and terrain resolution vary. No tree geometry is added; satellite ground textures can still show forest. Six-foot ground clearance depends on the source elevation model.
Estimated night lighting
Illuminated windows, moonlight brightness, and regional skyglow are models, not measured reproductions. Optional NASA Black Marble imagery supplies broad city-light patterns. City brightness changes estimated lighting and skyglow, affecting faint-star visibility. Display brightness and your own dark adaptation affect appearance.
Astronomical sky
Real J2000 catalog stars transformed to the selected date and location. Moon position and phase follow the date; the slider overrides illumination until the date changes or Auto is selected. A Moon below the horizon supplies no direct light. Standard celestial refraction is included; variable terrestrial refraction and weather are not.
Data and detail
Each dataset has a detail setting in Data & quality. Finer source tiles are requested where available; supersampling improves display edges. Interpolation smooths existing data without adding measured detail. Source coverage, resolution, graphics memory, and network speed still limit the view.
Lens presets
Rectilinear, 36 mm full-frame equivalent focal lengths. The naked-eye preset is a 60° horizontal viewing window. Perceived 1× size depends on your screen and viewing distance. Zoom changes framing, with the same naked-eye sensitivity; it adds no telescope light gathering or long-exposure effect.
Date and time
Pacific time with automatic PST/PDT. Buildings and night-light imagery use the available dataset vintage; selecting a historical date changes the sky, not the city’s construction history. The repeated autumn hour uses its first occurrence.
